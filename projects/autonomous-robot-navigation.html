Autonomous Robot Mapping & Navigation

ROS 2Nav2DockerLIDARJetson

During my internship at Hellbender, I worked alongside another intern to develop an autonomous navigation system for a differential-drive robot capable of mapping and navigating hallways and lab spaces while avoiding obstacles.

Using ROS 2 and the Nav2 stack, I integrated sensors including LIDAR, motor encoders, IMU, and a camera, and tuned navigation parameters for reliable obstacle avoidance and path planning. We explored mapping options such as SLAM Toolbox and visual SLAM frameworks to evaluate their performance in indoor environments.

This project required coordinating multiple computing platforms, including Raspberry Pi and NVIDIA Jetson devices, communicating over both Ethernet and Wi-Fi. I also performed board-level validation (verifying power, resistances, CAN bus communication, and Ethernet performance) to ensure hardware reliability.

Along the way, I addressed several real-world challenges:

  • Resolved oscillation at end-goals by switching controllers and tuning parameters
  • Improved stability on a larger robot platform by increasing controller lookahead distance
  • Mitigated time-sync issues and human error across devices by implementing Docker containers with automated startup scripts, allowing the full robot stack to be launched from a single desktop icon
  • Reduced odometry drift by tuning odometry and mapping parameters.

After iterative testing and integration, the robot successfully navigated ~80 meters of hallway and lab environments autonomously.

Through this project, I gained hands-on experience with ROS 2 navigation (Nav2), autonomous mapping, and sensor integration. I developed practical skills in controller tuning, adapting algorithms to real-world behavior, and Docker-based deployment for automation.
