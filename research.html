Engineering education research on mathematical modeling.

2024

When Is It Relevant?

I contributed to a research paper analyzing first-year engineering students’ perceptions of statistical variability in mathematical modeling. Through a collective autoethnographic study, we found that students’ sense of relevance toward variability develops differently depending on personal and academic experiences. The work highlights how fostering relevance can improve engineering education and demonstrates methods for ethically validating student-centered research.

Bostwick, L., A. George, T. Lee, and Z. del Rosario (2024). When Is It Relevant? A Collaborative Autoethnography Study by Engineering Students on Statistical Variability. In: ASEE Annual Conference and Exposition.

2025

Student Voice in Collaborative AutoEthnography

I co-authored a research paper exploring how sustained collaborative autoethnography (CAE) in mathematical modeling supports both student learning and authentic research. We demonstrated that engaging students in CAE fosters metacognition and reflective practice—key elements of mindset development—while also elevating student voices to strengthen research outcomes.

George, A., L. Bostwick, A. Renaud, E. Boyd, and Z. del Rosario (2025). Research as Teaching: On Student Mindset and Voice in a Sustained Collaborative AutoEthnography on Mathematical Modeling. In: ASEE Annual Conference and Exposition.
